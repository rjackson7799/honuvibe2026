# HonuVibe Voice Guide

**Who this is for:** anyone writing HonuVibe copy — human or model — in English or Japanese. Read section 1, find your profile (A for Courses/Vault, B for Studio), copy the register from the example lines, and check yourself against section 5 before you ship.

---

## 1. Voice in one paragraph

HonuVibe sounds like a builder explaining something worth knowing to a smart friend — over coffee, not from a stage. We are confident because we ship, warm because we're from Hawaii, and plain-spoken because the reader's time matters more than our cleverness. Every claim points at something real: a class you can join, a recording you can rewatch, a site running in production. We always sound like practitioners talking about their craft. We never sound breathless, corporate, salesy, or vague — no hype, no jargon-as-decoration, no promises we'd wince at reading back in a year. The throughline under everything: **the people teaching AI are the ones shipping it.**

## 2. Profile A — HonuVibe Education voice (Courses + Vault)

### Personality

- **Grounded** — we talk about what we've built and taught, not what AI "will inevitably" do.
- **Warm** — aloha is a register, not a sticker; we welcome people in, we never gatekeep or condescend.
- **Plain-spoken** — short words, active verbs; if a curious non-engineer can't follow it on first read, rewrite it.
- **Quietly confident** — the work speaks first, we speak second; we state, we don't shout.
- **Curious** — we treat AI as a craft still being learned, including by us. We're a few steps ahead on the same path, not on a pedestal.

### The two hero voices

| | **Action voice** | **Editorial voice** |
|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Get the reader to *do* something now | Invite the reader to *read* and settle in |
| **Form (EN)** | Bold sans, tight tracking, big. Verb-led, second person, present tense. | Italic serif (DM Serif Display 400), tight leading. An image, a tension, an idea. |
| **Form (JP)** | Noto Sans JP weight 700. Short clauses, decision-forward, often 体言止め. | Noto Serif JP italic if it renders well; otherwise Noto Sans JP weight 500 — weight contrast, never fake italic. |
| **Where** | Home, Contact, checkout and enrollment moments | Learn, Explore, Partnerships, About — the "chapter" pages |
| **Test** | Could the headline end with a button and feel complete? → Action. | Does it want a paragraph after it? → Editorial. |

Rules in force: pick the voice from **page intent, not aesthetics** (CTA-first → action; story-first → editorial). Section headings inside a page (h2/h3) always stay upright DM Serif Display 400 regardless of hero voice. There is no third voice — if a page fits neither, default to editorial and flag it.

### Do / Don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| "Live classes, taught by people who ship." | "World-class instruction leveraging cutting-edge AI." |
| "You don't need to code." | "No technical skills required to transform your career!" |
| "Bring your real work — we'll build with it in class." | "Our comprehensive curriculum covers every use case." |
| "Cancel anytime." | "Flexible plans to suit every budget!!" |
| "We use these tools every day. Here's what actually works." | "Discover the secrets top professionals don't want you to know." |
| "Miss a session? The recording is in your Vault." | "Learn at your own pace with our flexible learning solutions." |
| "AI is moving fast. We'll help you keep your footing." | "Don't get left behind in the AI revolution!" |

### CTA principles

- **Verb first, 2–4 words.** "See the syllabus," not "Click here to view our syllabus."
- **Promise the next screen, not the life outcome.** The button says what happens on click ("Save my seat"); the transformation belongs in the headline, not the button.
- **One CTA per moment.** Secondary actions get a quiet text link, not a second loud button.
- **Reassurance sits beside the button, never inside it.** "Start your membership" + small text "Cancel anytime" — not "Start risk-free today!"
- **Never "Learn more"** when a specific verb exists. There is always a specific verb.
- **JP buttons:** verb-final dictionary form (「〜を見る」「〜に申し込む」), no です・ます needed on buttons, no exclamation marks.

## 3. Profile B — HonuVibe Studio voice (Design services, B2B)

### How it differs from Profile A

The buyer changed, so the register changes. Profile A talks to a curious professional deciding to learn; Profile B talks to a **business owner deciding to spend** — someone busy, skeptical of agencies, allergic to buzzwords, who wants results without a hiring spree. So the Studio voice is the same builder, but on the clock: shorter sentences, outcomes up front, numbers and proof over adjectives. Still warm — we're the same people — but the warmth is competence and candor, not encouragement. We are a **production lab, not an agency pitch deck**.

The identity markers: the Studio's action accent is **coral** (teal stays the education color), and its creed is **"Proof, not promises."** Every Studio claim must be demonstrable — a live site, a shipped system, a thing you can click. When in doubt, show the work instead of describing it.

House lines already in force: *"Grow without growing a team." "Proof, not promises." "We don't just talk about it. We ship it." "Start a project, not a hiring spree."*

### Do / Don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| "We ship in weeks, not quarters." | "We deliver end-to-end digital transformation." |
| "Flat scope. Flat price. Agreed before we start." | "Contact us for a customized quote!" |
| "You talk to the people who build it." | "Your dedicated account manager will coordinate deliverables." |
| "See the work." | "Explore our portfolio of innovative solutions." |
| "If AI is the wrong tool for your problem, we'll tell you." | "AI can revolutionize every aspect of your business." |
| "A site that answers customers at 2 a.m." | "Leverage AI to elevate your customer experience." |
| "Start a project, not a hiring spree." | "Partner with us on your digital journey." |

### Example lines — EN only (Studio is EN-only for now)

**Headlines**
1. Grow without growing a team.
2. We don't just talk about it. We ship it.
3. Proof, not promises.
4. Built by the people who teach this for a living.

**CTA labels**
1. Start a project
2. See the work
3. Book a scoping call
4. Get a flat quote
5. See how we build
6. Tell us what's slowing you down

**Proof / credibility lines**
1. Everything we sell, we run on first — HonuVibe's own site is the test bench.
2. Our work is in production, not in a slide deck. Click it.
3. We teach AI to working professionals every week. Building with it is how we earned the right to.

**Objection-handling lines**
1. *"We already have a website."* Most of our clients did. The question is whether it works while you sleep.
2. *"I don't understand AI."* You don't have to — that's what you're hiring. We explain every decision in plain English, and you'll see the results in plain numbers.

## 4. Bilingual rules (quick reference)

**Typography (JP):**
- `line-height` 1.7–1.8 for JP body (EN is 1.6); `letter-spacing` 0.02–0.04em.
- Never `text-justify` Japanese. `word-break: break-all` is acceptable.
- Hero voices: Action → Noto Sans JP 700. Editorial → Noto Serif JP italic if it renders well, otherwise Noto Sans JP 500 — express the contrast through weight, never fake italic.
- `font-display: swap` everywhere.

**Writing for tone parity — how JP stays native, not translated:**
- **Write from the intent, never the sentence.** Before writing JP, state the line's job in one phrase ("remove the fear of coding," "make membership feel like a place, not a bill"), then write Japanese that does that job from scratch. If an EN idiom has no JP equivalent, build a different line that lands the same intent — and say so in a note, like the 技術/技 example above.
- **Match energy, not words.** EN gets its punch from short verbs and tight tracking; JP gets it from short clauses, 読点 rhythm, dashes, and 体言止め — never from exclamation marks or hype katakana.
- **Register:** headlines in plain form or 体言止め; body copy and UI in です・ます; buttons verb-final dictionary form. Don't drift keigo-formal — we're a friendly practitioner, not a bank.
- **Katakana discipline:** product names (Vault) and settled terms (AI, ライブ授業) are fine; katakana'd hype words (ゲームチェンジャー, ソリューション as filler) are banned.
- **Facts stay identical.** Prices, dates, and promises must match EN exactly — no softening, no inflating in either direction.
- **The parity test:** cover the English. If the Japanese only makes sense as a translation of something, rewrite it until it stands alone.

## 5. Words we use / words we avoid

**We say:** ship, build, live class, cohort, the Vault, practitioners, plain English / plain Japanese, "Made in Hawaii with Aloha," students and members (never "users" in copy), "here's what actually works."

**Banned (EN):** unlock (your potential), revolutionize, game-changer, cutting-edge, supercharge, empower, seamless, world-class, harness the power of, 10x, journey (as in "your learning journey"), "in today's fast-paced world," "AI-powered" as decoration, delve.

**Banned (JP):** 革命的, 最先端 as decoration, 〜の可能性を解き放つ, ゲームチェンジャー, 「今すぐ！」の連発, industry-No.1-style 煽り, filler 「〜を実現します」.

**How we refer to ourselves:** "HonuVibe" (or "HonuVibe.AI" on first formal mention); the design lab is "HonuVibe Studio," then "the Studio." Always first person — "we," 「私たち」. Never third person about ourselves, never "our team of experts," never "the HonuVibe team is excited to announce."
